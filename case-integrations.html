Timeline10 Weeks

Turning a complex catalog of 40+ connectors into a clear, guided, and reusable experience.

Overview

An app marketplace is most powerful when it talks to the tools marketing teams already use. But for most customers, connecting those tools was intimidating. Setup could be complex, and every new integration required starting from scratch.

Over 10 weeks, I designed the end-to-end integrations experience: from how customers discover connectors to how they configure, manage, and reuse them across their organization.

The Challenge

A catalog that didn’t exist and integration templates dying to be found

The challenge? Support 40+ integrations across multiple categories. Start with a catalog of integrations. Make it valuable. Then, we integrate. Keeping in mind the user’s need for information to make them successful, a progress indicator, and all this repeatable, dynamic, and smart.

The risk? Driving churn if I couldn’t create a painless experience that provided our users with immense value.

The Opportunity

Turn integrations from a technical hurdle into a product strength

The connectors themselves were good. Google Analytics, Salesforce, Snowflake, Shopify. These were the tools our users were already running their businesses on. A well-designed integration experience could make this tool feel like the natural center of a marketing tech stack rather than one more tool to maintain.

The opportunity was to design the catalog and configuration experience around two things customers actually needed.

Discovery helps customers quickly find the connector for their use case, understand what it does, and know exactly what they needed to be confident.

Confidence gives customers the information and guardrails to complete a configuration successfully on their own, without support intervention.

Design Approach

Informative and intuitive

Discover → Configure → Manage

Discover

The catalog uses brand recognition as a way to quickly locate the integration users are looking for. Each connector card surfaces the information customers need before they start: category and primary use case, a plain-language description of what it does, how much setup work is ahead of them, and whether it’s Acoustic-managed or self-managed.

Configure

The configuration experience was built as a guided, step-by-step flow with progress visibility, inline validation, and integration-specific help. Contextual help on each step includes guidance specific to the integration type.

Manage

Templates let customers save a completed configuration as a named template and apply it to future integrations of the same type. Connection health at a glance shows the status of every integration: connected, needs attention, or error.

Key UX Principles

Solutions

From friction point to product differentiator

Catalog design

40+ integrations organized so customers can find the right connector quickly.

Guided setup

Step-by-step configuration with progress visibility, inline validation, and integration-specific help. No pointless errors.

Template system

Save and reuse any configuration. Making implementation a week’s worth of work versus a month.

Health at a glance

Connection status visible in the catalog view. Customers know immediately which integrations need attention without opening each one.

Right expectations early

Acoustic-managed vs. self-managed labels on every card tell customers how much work is ahead before they start.

Setup for success

Before you begin page to make sure the user has what they need to successfully complete the integration.

Outcome
This experience turns an integration catalog from a source of support tickets into a demonstration of how well-connected the platform is.

Instead of: Sea of tiles → no context → complex setup → support ticket
Intended experience: Clear card → guided setup → connected and running
Project Impact

Integration-related churn avoided and a catalog that sells

The integrations page became a featured section in sales demos. A well-designed catalog of 40+ recognizable connectors turns out to be a compelling argument for platform breadth.

The design pieces that moved the numbers most:

  • Brand-first catalog organization
  • Setting the right expectations up front
  • Inline validation in the configuration flow
  • The template system
