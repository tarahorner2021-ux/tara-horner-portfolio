Overview

An app marketplace is most powerful when it talks to the tools marketing teams already use. But for most customers, connecting those tools was intimidating. Setup could be complex, and every new integration required starting from scratch.

Over 10 weeks, I designed the end-to-end integrations experience: from how customers discover connectors to how they configure, manage, and reuse them across their organization.

The challenge

A catalog that didn't exist and integration template dying to be found

The challenge? Support 40+ integrations across multiple categories.

Start with a catalog of integrations. Make it valuable. Then, we integrate. Keeping in mind the user's need or information to make them successful, a progress indicator, and all this repeatable, dynamic, and smart.

The risk? Driving churn if I couldn't create a painless experience that provided our users with emmence value.

The opportunity

Turn integrations from a technical hurdle into a product strength

The connectors themselves were good. Google Analytics, Salesforce, Snowflake, Shopify, these were the tools our users were already running their businesses on. A well-designed integration experience could make this tool feel like the natural center of a marketing tech stack rather than one more tool to maintain.

The opportunity was to design the catalog and configuration experience around two things customers actually needed:

Discovery — help customers quickly find the connector for their use case, understand what it does, and know exactly what they needed to be able to connect.

Confidence — give customers the information and guardrails to complete a configuration successfully on their own, without support intervention.

Design approach

Informative and intuitive

Discover

The catalog uses brand recognition as a way to quickly locate the integration users are looking for. Each connector card surfaces the information customers need before they start:

  • The integration's category and primary use case
  • A plain-language description of what it does
  • Whether it's Acoustic-managed or self-managed, a distinction that tells customers how much setup work is ahead of them
  • Connection status for integrations already configured

Configure

The configuration experience was built as a guided, step-by-step flow with three key pieces:

Progress visibility — where customers can see where they are in the setup process and how many steps remain. Partial configurations are saved automatically, so an interruption doesn't mean starting over.

Inline validation — field-level feedback surfaces errors as they happen, with plain-language instructions for how to resolve them. No more submitting a form and receiving a generic error at the end.

Contextual help — each step includes guidance specific to the integration type. Configuring a Salesforce CRM connection shows different helper content than configuring a Snowflake data warehouse.

Manage

For teams managing multiple integrations, especially across the same category, we introduced two features that addressed this directly:

Templates — customers can save a completed configuration as a named template and apply it to future integrations of the same type. For a team running three Salesforce environments, this eliminates hours of repeated setup work.

Connection health at a glance — the catalog view shows the status of every active integration: connected, needs attention, or error. Customers no longer need to open each integration individually to check if their data is flowing.
