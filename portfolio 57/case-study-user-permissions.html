Overview

A major enterprise customer was at risk of churning because we lacked the user permission and brand customization controls their admins and marketers needed. I had 4 weeks to ship a complete administration suite or lose the account.

The result was a full redesign of the user management experience: a role-based permission matrix across four functional areas (Marketing & campaigns, Content & assets, Administration, Analytics & Reporting), a guided role creation and editing flow, three onboarding paths for bringing teams in, and a brand settings composer scoped to role-based access.

What started as a retention problem became one of the product's most capable enterprise features.

The challenge

Enterprise-grade control that didn't exist yet

Enterprise customers operate under strict access control requirements. Teams need to define exactly who can read, create, update, or delete within each area of the product complete with audit trails when something changes.

We had basic user management; you could add users and assign broad roles. What we didn't have was the granular, matrix-based permission system an enterprise security team would accept.

The complexity was real. A well-designed permission system has to:

  • Be comprehensive enough to satisfy a security admin with strict compliance requirements.
  • Be legible enough for an IT generalist who doesn't use the product daily.
  • Show the downstream consequences of any permission change before it's applied.
  • Scale to an organization with dozens of users across multiple roles.
  • Include a path for bulk user import .

The opportunity

Compose control, don't configure it

The insight that shaped the entire design direction: the people configuring permissions are not the same people who use the product every day. An IT admin setting up roles for a marketing team needs to understand what those permissions mean in practice.

The design opportunity was to create a permission system that communicated consequence. Not just "check this box to allow Create" but "here is what a Campaign Manager can and cannot do across every area of the product."

The goal became to make enterprise-grade access control feel like a visual configuration tool, not a spreadsheet of toggles.

Design approach

Four surfaces, one coherent system

User management

The user management home serves as the central view for administrators; a table of all users, their assigned roles, and quick access to edit or remove. Three states were designed to cover the full admin life-cycle:

  • Default state — full user list with role assignments, search, and inline actions
  • Empty state — a guided onboarding view offering three paths: add manually, import from CSV, or sync from SSO
  • Search state — filtered results with clear visual feedback on what's being searched

Role management

The role management view gives administrators a bird's-eye view of every role in the organization , who holds it, what tags are associated with it, and whether it's a standard or custom role.

Creating or editing a role opens a slide-in panel that keeps the role list visible in context. Admins can see all existing roles while building a new one, which prevents duplication and helps them understand how a new role fits the existing structure.

The centerpiece of the system is the permission matrix . A structured grid that maps every functional area against four access levels: Read only, Create, Update, Delete. Each cell is a checkbox, but the matrix is designed to be read as a whole. An admin reviewing a role can immediately see: this Campaign Manager can create and update campaigns, read content, and has no access to administration.

Brand control as a permission

Brand settings are governed by the same role framework as data access; who can see, who can change, and who can lock brand tokens is an admin decision.

Project impact

Churn prevented. A new enterprise capability shipped.

The at-risk account renewed. The permissions suite shipped within the 4-week window and immediately became a pillar of our sales story. A feature that hadn't existed became something sales teams could demo with confidence.

My design decisions that had the most impact:

  • The permission matrix. Security teams could evaluate a role's full access footprint.
  • Composable add-on roles reduced the number of custom roles organizations needed to create and maintain.
  • The CSV import path unblocked onboarding for large organizations with existing user directories.
  • Brand settings as a governed permission answered a recurring sales question about white-labeling.
