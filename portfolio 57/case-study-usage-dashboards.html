Overview

Acoustic Composer, the platform's AI writing assistant, runs on a monthly token allotment. Admins needed a way to see how their org was tracking against that allotment, and both admins and individual users needed a way to act before it ran out, not just a message after it already had.

I designed the usage dashboard around three states tied to how close an org actually is to its cap, and built two ways to refill an allotment early: a one-click extend for admins watching the whole org, and an in-product AI assistant that can walk an individual user through requesting more.

The challenge

A hard stop is a bad surprise

The existing design showed one number, tokens used out of tokens allotted, with no sense of trajectory. An org burning through its allotment at a dangerous pace looked identical to one cruising comfortably toward its reset date, until the moment it wasn't: Composer would simply stop working mid-period, with a support ticket as the first signal anyone had a problem.

That hit two different people differently. An admin managing the org's whole allotment needed to see it coming across every team. An individual marketer mid-task needed to know their own request wouldn't be the one that got cut off.

The opportunity

Warn early, and make the fix one click away

The opportunity was to turn a single flat number into a trajectory: on pace, approaching cap, or at cap, each with its own color, its own banner copy, and its own next action, so the dashboard's state always matched what actually needed to happen. Approaching-cap alerts were set to fire at 80% usage rather than 100%, giving an org real runway to act instead of a warning that arrives with the door already closing.

And since a warning by itself doesn't refill anything, every stage past "on pace" carries its own way out: an Extend action an admin can click straight from the org list, and an AI assistant that can walk an individual user through requesting a larger allotment or customizing when their own alert fires, before their next request is the one that gets stopped.

Design approach

Three stages, one dashboard

On pace

The default state reads calm on purpose: a purple banner instead of a warning color, "on pace" instead of a percentage, and a cap-status card that explains the math in plain language rather than just showing a number.

  • A cap-hit message preview sits on every dashboard, so an admin can see exactly what their team would see if they ever did hit the limit, before it happens, not after
  • Token consumption, period-over-period, and input/output split are all visible without digging, so trajectory is obvious at a glance

Approaching cap

At 80% usage the banner itself changes color, the headline changes to name the state directly, "Composer usage approaching cap," and the primary action becomes Extend now instead of a passive report link.

  • The vs-prior-period card gets an amber border and a projected total that's flagged "over cap" outright, turning a trend line into an early warning
  • The cap-status card explains what already happened behind the scenes: an 80% threshold telemetry event fired to the ops team, so the admin knows they're not the only one who's seen it

At cap

For Acoustic's own operations team, the same logic scales to every org at once: a cross-org table flags any org at or above 80% in amber and any org that's actually hit its cap in red, sorted so the most urgent rows surface first.

  • Stat cards up top answer the only three questions ops actually asks: how many orgs are close, how many are already stuck, how many tokens has the whole platform burned
  • Every flagged row carries its own one-click Extend button, so fixing an at-cap org never requires leaving the table

Key UX principles

Solutions

A dashboard that shows where you're headed, and an assistant that can act on it

The refill has to be as easy to find as the warning

A dashboard that only tells an admin they're about to run out, without a fast way to do something about it, just moves the anxiety earlier. Every approaching-cap or at-cap state carries its own action, not just its own color.

Cap status

On pace.

60% of monthly Composer allotment used with 9 days remaining. Approaching-cap alerts fire at 80% with a full stop at 100%.

Add credits

Trajectory over a raw number

"60% used · on pace · 9 days left" says more than "600K / 1M tokens" ever could: the dashboard states a conclusion, not just a measurement.

Warn at 80%, not 100%

The approaching-cap threshold fires early enough that an admin has real runway to act, instead of a warning that arrives at the same moment as the outage.

Every warning ships with an action

Extend now, View details, and the assistant's request-more link all sit right next to the state that triggered them, never a separate page to go find.

Preview the worst case, safely

The cap-hit message preview lets an admin see exactly what their team would see at 100%, without anyone actually having to hit the cap first.

One dataset, two audiences

The same usage numbers power an org-wide admin view and a personal, per-user view, each surfacing only the comparison that audience actually needs.

Project impact

From a hard stop to an early, actionable warning

The three-stage model gives every audience, an individual marketer, an org admin, Acoustic's own operations team, a dashboard that matches its urgency to what's actually happening, instead of one flat number that looks the same right up until it doesn't.

Pairing every warning with a refill path, an Extend button for admins and an AI-assisted request for individual users, means the moment someone learns they're close to their cap is also the moment they can do something about it.
