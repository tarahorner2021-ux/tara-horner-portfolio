Overview

Acoustic Connect ships new data integrations on a rolling basis — an AWS S3 sync, a native AI assistant built on MCP, a Microsoft Dynamics 365 bridge — and each one needs a launch moment that explains itself in a few seconds, on a page sales and marketing can actually point people to.

Alongside those launches, the in-product assistant itself had a smaller, quieter gap: nothing in the chat interface signaled that an AI, not a static system message, was on the other end of a response.

I designed the hero pattern behind three integration launches, and gave the assistant a visual mark of its own — a small diamond that pulses while it's active.

The challenge

Three launches, no family resemblance

Each integration had been treated as its own one-off: a headline, a screenshot, whatever call to action felt right that week, restyled from the last one. Nothing held them together as the same product, and a prospect looking at two integration pages side by side wouldn't necessarily recognize them as related.

The assistant had a version of the same problem. Its responses rendered in the same plain bubble as a form-validation message or an error state — nothing in the interface marked a message as the AI actually talking.

The opportunity

One pattern, flexible enough to carry three different stories

The opportunity was a single hero pattern built to flex across very different integrations — an infrastructure sync, an AI feature, a CRM bridge — without reading as a template stretched three ways: a headline that states the specific value, one plain-language line of what it actually does, a call to action matched to how technical the audience is, and a supporting visual that changes shape per integration while everything around it stays fixed.

For the assistant, the opportunity was smaller but just as concrete: give it a mark, not a full loading state. A pulse reads as "I'm here and thinking" without taking over the conversation the way a spinner or a "typing…" label does.

Design approach

Three integrations, one hero pattern

AWS S3

The most technical of the three, so the hero leads with the specific outcome, "Your AWS data, live in Connect," and closes with a specific action: "Connect your S3 bucket" instead of a generic "Get started."

  • An icon row previews exactly what starts flowing in — audience, consent, products, behavioral signals
  • The supporting visual is the AWS icon itself, so the audience it's for recognizes the integration at a glance

AI assistant

Rather than a static screenshot, the hero shows a live-looking exchange: a question typed in, a real answer with real numbers, a natural follow-up already queued. The value reads before anyone signs up.

  • A stat strip underneath grounds the preview in the platform's own numbers — audiences, data sources, campaign ROI, reach, live sessions
  • The call to action stays general, "Get started," since the next step is a conversation, not a config screen

Dynamics 365

A CRM bridge is the least visual of the three integrations, so the hero borrows motion instead: three feature tags scattered at a slight tilt, each with its own small icon, standing in for a product screenshot that wouldn't have said much on its own.

  • The Microsoft Dynamics 365 mark sits opposite the Acoustic Connect mark, naming both sides of the bridge up front
  • The call to action, "Connect now," matches the general audience of a CRM integration page

Key UX principles

Solutions

One pattern that flexes by integration, and a mark that gives the assistant a presence

A mark that says "I'm here," not "wait"

The assistant didn't need a full loading state to feel alive, just enough motion to read as present. A gentle pulse says that without asking for the user's attention the way a spinner or a "typing…" label would.

The mark, live

Same diamond used next to every assistant response, animating here the way it does in the product.

One pattern, three shapes

Headline, one plain-language value line, a call to action, and a supporting visual repeat across all three launches — only the supporting visual changes shape per integration.

A CTA matched to the audience

"Connect your S3 bucket" asks for the specific, technical action that page needs; "Get started" and "Connect now" stay general where the next step is a conversation, not a config screen.

No jargon in the headline

"Your AWS data, live in Connect" and "Bridge your CRM data to marketing audiences" say what happens, not what it's built on.

A live preview over a screenshot

The assistant's hero shows a real-looking exchange instead of a static product shot, so its value reads before anyone signs up.

The assistant gets a face

The diamond mark sits next to every assistant response, in the same place a person's own avatar would sit next to theirs.

Project impact

Three launches that now read as one product

The three launch pages share a single pattern now, so a new integration ships as a content problem, not a design problem: drop in a headline, a value line, and a supporting visual, and the rest of the page already works.

The assistant's mark did something smaller but more immediate — a chat that used to read like a transcript now has something visibly alive on the other end of it.
