Overview Pulse is the first mobile app in Acoustic’s portfolio. Marketing leaders and campaign managers needed a way to stay close to their customers’ live behavioral signals, in order to facilitate decisive action quickly. Over 14 weeks, I designed the end-to-end mobile experience. The goal wasn’t to put the full desktop product on a smaller screen. It was to answer a single question: what does a marketing leader need to know or do in the next 30 seconds? ￼ ￼ ￼ Get Briefed · Resonate AI · Explore audience The Challenge The most valuable signals were stuck on a desktop Acoustic’s platform detects live behavioral signals around the clock: product interest spikes, audience fatigue, browse abandonment surges, rage clicks. A marketing leader away from their computer had no way to know about or respond to an issue on-the-go. This created a real opportunity. The Opportunity A focused companion, not a shrunken desktop Acoustic already generated a plain-language summary of the most important signals detected. On desktop, it was one card in a busy interface. On mobile, it could be the entire starting point. The design opportunity was clear: build a mobile experience around the signals and actions that matter most to a marketing leader on the move, and make actionable insights available anywhere. Product Vision Three modes for a marketer on the move Get briefed → Explore → Act Get briefed Every session starts with a daily summary. Rather than opening to a redundant dashboard, the marketer immediately knows which revenue categories are leading or lagging, and total audience size and week-over-week shifts. The brief is designed to be read in under 30 seconds: enough to walk into a meeting informed, or decide whether a signal needs immediate action. Explore When a signal in the brief deserves a closer look, the marketer can move directly into the Explore layer without losing context. The app surfaces the full audience picture across key views: Segments, Contacts, Campaign Performance, Behavioral Trends. Act The most important interaction I designed for the mobile app was the AI assistant, built for short-session, on-the-go use. A marketer who spots an at-risk segment in the brief can immediately open the chat and ask why browse abandonment is spiking. The assistant explains the behavioral pattern, surfaces the relevant audience data, and offers to draft a recovery email subject line on the spot. Key UX Principles Design decisions A marketing colleague in your pocket. Lead with the Brief The AI brief is the homepage of the app. Not a dashboard, not a feed. The most important signals before the marketer has to search for them. Earned personalization Customize Pulse gives each user control over which metrics appear first. The same app serves a CMO and a campaign manager differently. Mobile-first AI The AI assistant redesigned for short sessions: direct answers, quick-tap actions, and subject line drafting that fits a mobile workflow instead of a desktop one. Context without clutter Segment health, contact-level behavior, and campaign performance available in the Explore layer when surfaced progressively, never all at once. Signal to action Every signal in the brief connects directly to the relevant audience segment, contact list, or chat conversation. No dead ends, no hunting. Signal to action Draft subject lines, flag alerts, explore a contact. Every key next step reachable within two taps from the brief. Outcome Pulse shifts Acoustic from a platform marketers check at their desk to an intelligence layer they carry with them. Instead of: Signal detected → only visible on desktop → marketer finds out tomorrow Intended experience: Signal surfaces in the brief → marketer investigates → Resonate drafts the response → campaign goes out today Project Impact The first mobile app in Acoustic’s history, shipped in 14 weeks Pulse launched as Acoustic’s first ever mobile app. The design challenge wasn’t just building something that worked on a small screen. It was building something that made Acoustic’s most sophisticated capabilities feel immediately useful to a marketer with 30 seconds to spare.
